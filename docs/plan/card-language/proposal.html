Card language: before and after

Some questions on the new ZERAOS cards read like writing, not like something a person says out loud. These rewrites keep each card's meaning and thread, but phrase them the way you'd ask a colleague across a table.

The five rules behind the rewrites

  1. Say it like a person, not a page. StoryCorps' interview questions, refined over 20 years, are short and spoken: "Who has been the biggest influence on your life?", "What is your earliest memory?"
  2. One question per card. Many Oak questions asked two things ("…, and what helps you let go?"). In a 90-second answer, people answer the first half and lose the second. The second half is what a 🐿️ Squirrel follow-up is for.
  3. No idioms. "Went out on a limb", "rubbed off", "in their corner", "spurred you on", "made it click" can't be guessed from their words, and many of the room speak English alongside other languages. Plain-language guidance for second-language readers says to avoid idioms and unclear phrasal verbs.
  4. Concrete over abstract. Aron's "36 questions" study builds closeness with specific moments ("When did you last sing to yourself?"), not poetic phrases ("What has a hard season grown in you?").
  5. Short enough to read aloud in one breath. The question shows on the big screen and the player reads it out; long questions get re-read instead of answered.

Sources: StoryCorps Great Questions · Aron's 36 questions · Idioms and phrasal verbs in writing for global readers

Why each one changed

  • Idiom An idiom or phrasal verb whose meaning can't be guessed from its words 18
  • Two questions Two questions in one; people answer the first and lose the second 14
  • Abstract An abstract or poetic phrase; a concrete moment is easier to answer 16
  • Too long Longer than needed to read aloud in one breath 5
  • Not how people talk Grammatical, but not how someone would ask it across a table 20

Recommendation: accept all rewrites. Nothing about the rules changes; only the wording of 73 questions.
