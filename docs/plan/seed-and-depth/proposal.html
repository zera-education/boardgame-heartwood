Seeds as appetizers, and a depth sweep

Season 1 is the whole room's first taste of each other. These changes make every Seed a fun, easy appetizer that sets up the same card's deeper questions later, and fix the questions that sat at the wrong depth.

What makes a Seed a good appetizer

  1. Playful frame. A quick pick ("karaoke, the dance floor or quiz night?"), a rating ("six stars out of five"), a small confession ("secretly terrible at"), or a picture you can see.
  2. Answered in one breath, by anyone in the room, with no thinking time.
  3. Likely to get a laugh. Laughter in Season 1 is what makes Season 3 feel safe.
  4. A small version of the card's theme. The perfectionist towel-folder (Seed) becomes the person with standards nobody asked for (Oak). By Season 3 the room already has a light picture of everyone's values to build on.
  5. Still safe and plain. The earlier rules hold: one question, no idioms, nothing exposing.

Aron's "36 questions" study opens the same way, with light, fun questions before deeper ones ("When did you last sing to yourself?").

How a question's depth is judged (the onion test)

Test🌱 Seed🌿 Sapling🌳 Oak
What you revealA preference, habit or light factOne experience from your lifeYour inner self: belief, motive, fear, hope, struggle
TimeAnythingThe past: a finished storyNow or future: still open
Risk if your boss hearsNoneLow: you pick the storyReal: it shows something unfinished
Quick checkA stranger at a bus stop could ask itYou'd tell a colleague over lunchYou'd say it only to someone you trust

Plus two rules: each card climbs (its Oak is deeper than its Sapling), and an Oak is about you, not your opinion of others. Based on social penetration theory (Altman & Taylor): people open up in layers, from preferences to experiences to core values.

Recommendation: accept all. Only question wording changes; the rules and the number of cards stay the same.
