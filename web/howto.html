HEARTWOOD

How to play

A cooperative game for ZERA's school leaders: wake the forest together.

  • 6 to 12 players
  • One team: you win or lose together
  • A long afternoon (3 to 4 hours)
  • Phones down

1What this is

Heartwood is a game for getting to know each other while you work as one team. The forest is asleep. Together you explore it, grow a Big Tree for every value, tell stories when you harvest fruit, find three treasures, and gather on the World Tree in the centre. Then the forest wakes.

Nobody plays against anybody. There are no points and no winner among the players. The whole team wakes the forest, or the whole team runs out of water. So talk, plan out loud, and help each other.

Say it, and the Keeper taps it

Everything public happens on the big screen, the Keeper screen. When it is your turn, you say what you want to do ("I move into Resilience", "I explore here") and the Keeper taps it. The screen shows it: your medallions hop, hexes turn over, plants grow, and leaves blow in at the Forest Tide.

Your phone stays face down. It only shows private things: who you watch as a Secret Owl, your roles, what you carry, and what only you can see. You pick it up to give trust after a story, and to make your guess at the end.

ZERA's story is a tree's story: a seed planted in 2021 that grows into a forest by 2035. (Zera is Hebrew for seed, and our mascot is an acorn. In this game an acorn always means one thing: the trust you give each other.)

You, the players

6 to 12 school leaders. Each of you has your own phone.

  1. Open the game on your phone with the 4-letter code on the big screen, or scan its QR code.
  2. Type your name and pick a colour, then tap Join.
  3. Take your photo, or skip it. It shows in your round medallion on the big screen; with no photo, your initial shows. Your photo is deleted after the game ends.
  4. Wait in the lobby. The Keeper gives you your Enneagram roles and starts the game.

The Keeper

The facilitator. The Keeper is not a player. The Keeper runs the big screen, gives each player 1 to 3 Enneagram roles, starts the game, taps what players say, closes each story with Done sharing, can start a timer, and leads the finale.

The Keeper may pause a story at any time, and can give a player a rejoin code if their phone changes.

2The goal

The forest wakes the moment all three goals are true at the same time. The big screen shows how far the team has come.

  1. Goal 1 × 6A Big Tree in every sectorOne Big Tree in each of the six value sectors. It does not matter who grew it.
  2. Goal 2 + Fruit from everyone, and all three treasuresEvery player has placed at least one fruit on the World Tree, and the three treasures are there too.
  3. Goal 3 on Everyone on the World TreeEvery player stands on the World Tree, together.

How the team can lose

If every player is at 0 water at the same moment, the forest sleeps on and the game ends. Nobody is ever out of the game alone: a player with no water waits for a teammate to pass them some (see Water and springs).

Win or lose, the game goes on to the same finale: the Secret Owl guesses, the tribute chain, and recognition for everyone.

3The forest

The forest has 61 hexes: the World Tree in the centre and four rings around it. Six lines from the centre cut it into six sectors, one for each of ZERA's core values, ZERAOS, reading clockwise from the top.

  • ZealousBe zealous, not jealous.
  • ExcellenceBeyond expectation.
  • ResilienceAnti-fragile.
  • AuthenticityInclusive education.
  • Open-mindednessGrowth mindset.
  • SustainabilityStart with the end in mind.

Each sector has 10 hexes: 4 on Ring 4, 3 on Ring 3, 2 on Ring 2 and 1 on Ring 1.

What lies under the hexes

The World Tree is face up. The other 60 hexes start face down (). Nobody knows what is under each one until someone explores it:

  • 53 empty clearings. Good places to sow a seed.
  • 4 springs , anywhere in Rings 1 to 4. The only place to get water back.
  • 3 treasures , hidden only in Rings 1 and 2: the Compass, the Lantern and the Rope.

Any number of players can stand on the same hex.

4Setup and entering

Before the game

  1. The Keeper creates a game. 6 to 12 players join on their phones.
  2. In the lobby, the Keeper gives each player 1 to 3 Enneagram types. These are your roles. The game cannot start until every player has at least one.
  3. The Keeper starts the game with the briefing: slides on the big screen about the forest and its six values, the team's goal, what you have and its limits, what stands in your way (the weather, dead leaves, running dry) and how to prevent it, your roles, the Secret Owl side quest, the Forest Pact and how you play. The Keeper steps through them; phones stay down.
  4. On the roles slide your phone shows your roles. On the Secret Owl slide it shows the colleague you watch as their Secret Owl. Keep it to yourself.
  5. Each sector gets its first weather: Sun, Rain or Fog.

Water

You start with 4 water. You can hold 5 at most.

Fruit

You start with none. You can carry 2 at most.

Treasure

You start with none. You can carry 1 at most.

Trust acorns

You have 10 to give to others, for the whole game.

Entering the forest

In turn order, each player:

  1. Chooses the value they stand for. Several players may stand for the same value.
  2. Chooses a hex on Ring 4 of that value's sector. The Keeper places your medallion there. You may share a hex with someone.
  3. Tells the room why they stand for this value. The others listen and may give you a trust acorn.

You stand for your value. It is yours for the whole game. You will grow trees in other sectors too, and tell stories about other values. That is how you get to know what your colleagues stand for.

5A turn

Players take turns clockwise, in the order they joined. On your turn you have 2 actions. Say what you want to do, and the Keeper taps it. You may use fewer.

  1. 1First player: 2 actions
  2. →
  3. 2Next player: 2 actions
  4. → … →
  5. Last player: 2 actions
  6. →
  7. Forest Tide

When you are done, the Keeper taps End turn. After the last player, the Forest Tide runs by itself, and a new round begins.

Free, on your turn

Pass water, fruit or a treasure to a teammate on your hex. It costs no action, and you do it on your own turn. See Passing.

Placing on the World Tree is free and happens by itself. See Placing on the World Tree.

When someone shares, the forest waits

Stories come one at a time. While someone is sharing, nobody can act. When the story ends, the Keeper taps Done sharing and play goes on. If several stories are waiting, they come one after another.

Your roles can change these rules a little: for example, a Helper can water a hex next to them, and an Enthusiast can move 2 hexes once a turn.

6Water and springs

Water keeps you walking. You start with 4 and can hold 5. The big screen and your phone show how much you have.

You lose water when…

  • You Water a plant: 1 water each time.
  • The sun dries you at the Forest Tide: 1 water, if your sector is sunny and you are not standing on a Big Tree.

You get water back when…

  • You Drink at a spring: back to 5, for 1 action.
  • A teammate passes you water.

That is all. Rain does not fill you up.

Springs

Four springs hide under the face-down hexes, anywhere in the forest. When someone explores one, everyone can see it. A spring never runs dry: anyone can drink there, as often as they like.

At 0 water

You stay in the game. You cannot Move until a teammate comes to your hex and passes you 1 water on their turn. You can still do other actions on your hex, and you can still drink if you stand on a spring.

The team loses only if every player is at 0 water at the same moment. So keep an eye on each other. Tell the team where the springs are, and share your water before someone runs dry.

Harvest and the harvest story

Stand on a Big Tree and Harvest to take 1 fruit. Then you tell a story:

"Tell us a story from your own life about Resilience."

The value is the value of the sector the tree grows in, not your own value. The big screen shows that value's line too (for Resilience: Anti-fragile.). Listeners may give you a trust acorn.

  • Not in rain (fog is fine).
  • Each tree gives 1 fruit between Forest Tides.
  • You carry 2 fruit at most. Bring them to the World Tree.

8The three treasures

Three treasures hide under face-down hexes, only in Rings 1 and 2. Each one has a meaning for a team.

  1. Find it. When someone explores a treasure hex, the treasure appears on the big screen, and the room has a group moment.
  2. Take it. The treasure lies on its hex until a player stands there and takes it (1 action). You carry 1 treasure at most.
  3. Bring it home. Walk it to the World Tree, or pass it from teammate to teammate. It is placed by itself when it arrives.

The Compass

Purpose: where we're going.

"Everyone, one sentence: where do you want this team to be a year from now?"

The Lantern

Lighting the way for others.

"Everyone, one sentence: who lit the way for you when you were new?"

The Rope

Holding together.

"Everyone, one sentence: what holds this team together when things get hard?"

The group moment

When a treasure is found, everyone answers its question in one sentence, around the table. There are no trust acorns for a group moment: everyone gives something, together. Then the Keeper taps Done sharing.

When all three treasures are on the World Tree, the forest says: "Purpose, light and each other: the heart of the forest is complete."

9Placing on the World Tree

The World Tree is the centre of the forest. Bring your fruit and the treasures here.

  • It happens by itself. Whenever you stand on the World Tree holding fruit or a treasure, everything you carry is placed at once. It costs no action. It happens after you move there, when someone passes you something there, or when a Challenger brings you there.
  • One Heartwood question. If you placed at least one fruit, you answer one Heartwood question , however many fruit you placed. Listeners may give you a trust acorn.
  • Every player must place a fruit. A fruit counts for the person who places it. So if you have not placed one yet, a teammate can pass you a fruit, and you place it.
  • The World Tree has no weather, and dead leaves never land on it. You may walk off it again.

Heartwood questions (the game's own deck, named after the heart of a tree) are about this team and what you hope for. For example: "What has this team given you that you didn't expect?" or "Who at this table do you want to thank, and for what?" See them all in the wiki.

10Passing

Passing is free. On your own turn, when nobody is sharing, you can pass water, fruit or a treasure to a teammate. It costs no action. Only the player whose turn it is can give; everyone else waits for their own turn. Just say it, and the Keeper taps it.

  • Same hex: you can always pass to someone on your hex.
  • Limits: the teammate can hold 5 water, 2 fruit and 1 treasure at most.

The Peacemaker

A Peacemaker can pass across hexes: to, and from, a teammate on a hex next to theirs. Nobody else can.

Yes: A and the Peacemaker stand on hexes next to each other, so they can pass to each other, either way: each gives on their own turn.

No: A and C can't pass to each other through the Peacemaker. Each of them can pass with the Peacemaker.

11Weather

Each sector has its own weather: Sun, Rain or Fog. Each is equally likely. The weather is drawn at the start and again at every Forest Tide. The big screen colours each sector by its weather. The World Tree has no weather.

12The Forest Tide

After the last player's turn, the forest moves by itself. The big screen shows it step by step.

  1. 1 · New weatherEach sector turns to its new weather. Every tree can be harvested again.
  2. 2 · Rain grows, sun driesIn rainy sectors, Seeded and Sprout grow one step. In sunny sectors, every player who is not on a Big Tree loses 1 water (never below 0).
  3. 3 · Forest BreathDead leaves blow in from the edge and drift toward the centre.

Forest Breath and dead leaves

At every Tide, the wind picks a few random places: hexes of the forest, or spots just beyond its edge. From each one, one layer of dead leaves blows onto a hex next to it, one ring closer to the centre. From beyond the edge, the leaves land on Ring 4, so no ring is out of the wind. A Ring 1 hex keeps its own leaves. The World Tree never gets leaves.

Clearing

Clear removes 1 layer of dead leaves from your hex or a hex next to you. So you can open a sealed hex from outside. In rain it costs 2 actions. A Reformer removes 2 layers at once, for 1 action, even in rain.

Ring cards

Each ring has its own deck: Light, Story and Deep. The deeper the ring, the deeper the question:

  • Ring 3: Light. "What could you do for hours without noticing the time?"
  • Ring 2: Story. "Tell us about a hard time, big or small, that made you stronger."
  • Ring 1: Deep. "What makes you feel you truly belong somewhere?"

You draw a ring card only the first time you step into each ring, moving inward. So each player draws at most 3 ring cards in a game. If a Challenger brings you into a new ring, you draw too.

Trust acorns

You have 10 trust acorns for the whole game. After someone else shares, pick up your phone and tap Give trust if their story moved you. One acorn per story. You cannot give trust to yourself, and there is no trust after a group moment.

Trust is a gift, not a grade. It does not decide a winner. At the end, everyone sees how many acorns they received, and from how many people.

While someone else shares

  • Listen to understand. No advice, no fixing, no debating.
  • If it moved you, give trust.
  • Keep quietly watching the colleague you are the Secret Owl for.
  • The Keeper may start a timer as a gentle guide. When it runs out, finish your thought kindly.

Recording

Each story is recorded on the Keeper's laptop, one clip per share, and turned into text for the team on our own computer. The recordings stay with the team: only the Keeper can open them.

The Keeper can switch recording off for the whole game, or tap Don't keep this one while a story is told. Just ask.

14The nine roles

The Enneagram describes nine personality types, each with its own gift. In the lobby, the Keeper gives each player 1 to 3 types. These are your roles. Several players may have the same type.

Roles are always on: use them as often as you like. Your phone lists your roles as a reminder. When you use one, say it aloud ("As the Helper, I water the hex next to me"), so the Keeper can tap it.

  • 1

    Reformer

    gift: integrity

    When you Clear, remove 2 layers of dead leaves.

    Clearing costs you 1 action, even in rain.

  • 2

    Helper

    gift: care

    Water or Tend a hex next to you, without standing on it.

    Like everyone, you can also Clear a hex next to you.

  • 3

    Achiever

    gift: drive

    After you Sow, the hex grows straight to Sprout.

    This saves one Water action, and 1 water.

  • 4

    Individualist

    gift: depth

    When you Explore, you also peek at one face-down hex next to you.

    Only your phone shows what is there. Tell the team, or keep it for later.

  • 5

    Investigator

    gift: insight

    See which hexes the next Forest Breath will hit. Once a round, see one sector's next weather.

    Both show on your phone. The Breath is always there. The weather look is on your own turn, once a round, and costs no action.

  • 6

    Loyalist

    gift: loyalty

    Plants on your hex and on the 6 hexes around you (7 in all) are safe from dead leaves.

    Anyone keeps the plant they stand on safe; you also guard the ring around you. Leaves still land there, and two layers still seal the hex. But the plants do not drop a step.

  • 7

    Enthusiast

    gift: joy

    Once a turn, one Move may go 2 hexes, for 1 action.

    Not if your hex, the middle hex or the last hex is in fog. Not through sealed hexes.

  • 8

    Challenger

    gift: strength

    You may enter sealed hexes. When you Move, you may bring one teammate from your hex.

    The teammate moves with you. If they enter a new ring, they draw its card too.

  • 9

    Peacemaker

    gift: harmony

    You and a teammate on a hex next to yours can pass things to each other.

    See Passing.

15Secret Owl

At the start, your phone gives you one colleague's name: you are their Secret Owl. Tap to see it, and keep it to yourself. An owl watches quietly and misses nothing. All game, you watch them, while someone else quietly watches you.

All the names form one loop around the room. Everyone watches one person, and is watched by one person.

Notice three things about the colleague you watch: something they love, something that shaped them, something they hope for. Listen to their stories, talk with them as you plan, and watch how they help the team.

Watch without being obvious, or they will guess that you are their Owl.

Tip: be curious about everyone, so your interest in one person blends in.

16How it ends

The forest wakes

The moment all three goals are true, even in the middle of a turn, the forest wakes. The big screen celebrates: "The forest wakes!"

The forest sleeps on

If every player is at 0 water at the same moment, the game ends. The forest sleeps on, for another day.

Out of time? The Keeper can close the game early. The forest stays asleep, and the finale still happens.

The finale

Either way, the finale is the same. It is about the people, not the forest.

  1. Guess. On your phone, guess who was your Secret Owl. You can change your guess until the Keeper starts the tribute chain.
  2. The tribute chain. One at a time, around the loop, each Owl gives the colleague they watched about one minute:
    "Three things I discovered about you today, and one thing I appreciate."
    Then that colleague honours the person they watched, and so on, until the loop closes. The Keeper taps Next each time.
  3. Recognition. The big screen shows each person: how many trust acorns they received and from how many people, who their Secret Owl was, whether they guessed right, and whether they stayed hidden as an Owl.

There are no points and no winner among the players. Everyone in the room hears three true things someone noticed about them, and one thing someone appreciates.

17Your phone

What your phone shows

  • The colleague you watch as their Secret Owl.
  • What you carry, and which rings you have reached.
  • Your roles, as a reminder.
  • Your private looks: the Individualist's peek, the Investigator's Forest Breath and weather.
  • Your photo: retake it or remove it until the game ends.
  • Give trust, after someone else shares.
  • Your Secret Owl guess, at the end.

If your phone drops out

The small badge in the corner says ● Live while your phone is connected. If it says ● Reconnecting… or ● Offline, it catches up by itself once the Wi-Fi is back. Nothing you did is lost.

Closed the browser? Open the game again on the same phone and tap Continue as your name. On a different phone, ask the Keeper for a 4-digit rejoin code.

18The Forest Pact

The Keeper reads this at the start, on the briefing's Forest Pact slide. It holds for the whole game, and after it.

  1. What's shared in the forest stays in the forest.
  2. Every question is an invitation. You may always say "not this one".
  3. Listen to understand: no advice, no fixing, no debating.
  4. Trust is a gift, not a grade.
  5. One team. Plan out loud, and look after each other.
  6. Phones down. Pick yours up only to give trust, to check your private looks, and to guess.

The Keeper may pause any story.
